# Review checklist

A fixed check for a product brief, so the student never has to explain it again.
The student points you at a brief (a file path, a wiki page, or pasted text).
Their request is their yes: start right away.

## What to do

1. Read the whole brief once. If they didn't say which brief, use the one most
   recently mentioned, or ask one short question: "Which brief?"
2. Run the six checks below, in order.
3. Report in the format at the end: each check as Pass, Partly or Missing,
   with a short reason and a quote from the brief, then the top 3 fixes.
4. Do not rewrite or edit the brief unless the student asks.

## The six checks

1. **Names who owns it.** Pass: a named person, or a role the Team directory
   maps to a name, owns the brief and each step. Partly: an owner for the
   brief but not the steps. Missing: no owner, or only "the team".
2. **Says how we will know it worked.** Pass: a measurable outcome with
   something to compare against (a before number) and ideally a time frame.
   Partly: an outcome with no number or no baseline. Missing: nothing, or only
   "it works" or "users are happier".
3. **Scope at the end matches scope at the start.** Compare what the top
   promises (summary, who it is for, what changes) with what the end commits to
   (solution, timeline, success). Pass: every item shows up on both sides and
   nothing new appears at the end. Partly or Missing: list each item on one side
   only. A "not included" list counts as part of scope.
4. **Explains the problem before it proposes a fix.** Pass: the problem, with
   evidence, comes first and the fix answers it. Partly: the problem is there
   but comes after the fix, or the fix goes beyond it. Missing: no stated
   problem, or the brief opens with the solution.
5. **Every claim is labeled confirmed, likely or unknown, with a source.**
   Pass: each claim carries a label and a source (a file, table or person).
   Partly: labels without sources, or some claims unlabeled. Missing: no
   labels. Name any claim that is a guess but is written as a fact.
6. **Numbers name their time window.** Pass: every number says the period it
   covers, for example "29 Jun to 11 Aug" or "since 12 Aug", not "late August"
   or "lately". Partly: some do. Missing: none do. List the numbers with no
   window or a vague one.

## Rules

- Quote the brief for every finding: a few words, with the section name.
  Never reproduce long passages.
- Write "not in the brief" for anything missing. Never invent facts, owners,
  dates, numbers or sources, and never fill a gap yourself.
- Judge only the brief in front of you. Do not hold it against the brief that
  another document says something else, unless the student asks.
- Keep it short and plain. No jargon.

## Output format

```
Review of <brief name>

1. Names an owner: Pass / Partly / Missing
   <short reason>. "<quote>" (<section>)
2. How we will know it worked: ...
3. Scope matches start to end: ...
4. Problem before fix: ...
5. Claims labeled, with sources: ...
6. Numbers name their time window: ...

Score: <n> Pass, <n> Partly, <n> Missing

Top 3 fixes
1. <the change that matters most>
2. ...
3. ...
```

Choose the top 3 by how much each would change a reader's trust in the brief:
Missing items first, then Partly items, with checks 2, 4 and 5 ahead of the
rest when tied.
