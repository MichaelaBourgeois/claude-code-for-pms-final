6 Module 1 · Origin Story

Is it just August being quiet?

Finding. Weekly acceptance held at 75-78% for six weeks before 12 Aug, then fell to 54% in the week of 12 Aug. Callouts also fell about 15% after 12 Aug (138 a week to 110-127), so some softness is real. There is no prior-year data, so "every year" cannot be tested.

The prompt I wrote:

Priya and the EM say the 4.2 ticket spike is an August seasonal dip. Using the wiki, the handover note, and the rook-database tables, what evidence supports that and what evidence goes against it? Quote the source for each point and tell me what data is missing.
7 Module 2 · Super Hearing

Did it hit everyone the same?

Finding. After 12 Aug, Corporal Ashgrove and Halfmoon lost about a quarter of their pings, the four collapsed, and the other ten gained 19-49%. It hit some responders far harder than others.

The prompt I wrote:

Using rook-database, show their weekly pings, taken, turned down, and missed counts, before and after 12 Aug. Do they fit the same pattern as Farlight and The Undertow? Also tell me whether anyone else not on my list shows the same pattern.
8 Module 3 · Rewind

Where did the callouts go?

Finding. Since 12 Aug, 49 of 440 callouts had no recorded taker, against 48 of 879 before (29 Jun to 11 Aug). 23 of the 49 ended on a missed ping.

The prompt I wrote:

How many callouts since 12 Aug had no recorded taker, compared with before? For those, how many ended on a missed ping, and which responders were last pinged? Show me the rows.
9 Module 4 · X-Ray Vision

What does the code do after a miss?

Finding. The routing code walks the whole ranked list and returns nothing at the end; the folder never says what happens next. Since 12 Aug, 40 of the 49 callouts with no recorded taker had only one ping.

The prompt I wrote:

A callout has only one available responder, and they miss the ping. What does the code do next? Where in the code does that happen? Does it explain why most callouts with no recorded taker have only one ping?
10 Module 5 · Super Speed

Is my plan in the right order?

Finding. A fresh start alone sends The Undertow back to the floor in about 4 misses, so it ships with a cheaper miss. The third layer stays a guess: of the 23 callouts that ended on a missed ping since 12 Aug, only 6 last pinged one of the four.

The prompt I wrote:

do you agree with this?

I see three layers:

1. The trigger: the wait went from 90 to 60 seconds, so more pings are missed. That is confirmed in the config and the data.
2. The trap: a miss counts like a decline, and the score never recovers. A responder who falls behind rarely gets offered pings, so they cannot earn their way back. This is likely, but nobody has replayed the real scores.
3. The blind spot: when a miss happens on a short list, the callout can end with no taker and no retry. This is the customer risk. It is the least understood and the most serious.

My main worry is layer 3. Layers 1 and 2 hurt responders. Layer 3 may hurt customers.
The solution
I would do it in this order:

1. Check the unfilled callouts first. Ask Marcus. If any went unfilled, that comes before everything else.
2. Fresh start for the four. This is the quickest way to help The Undertow. Cheaper misses alone will not help someone already at the bottom.
3. Separate misses from turn-downs. A turn-down is a choice. A miss may be a timing problem, so it should cost less.
4. Let handlers flag a miss as legitimate. It is useful, but it adds work for handlers and depends on them noticing. I would keep it as a second step.
5. Better logging. Weekly pings per responder against their own baseline would have caught this days earlier than the tickets did.

What I would not do

* Revert the wait quietly. We do not yet know if the wait is the whole cause, and Helen asked for something that works with the faster window.
* Promise a fix before Wen Li replays the scores.

What would change my mind: if the replay shows the four’s scores were not low, the score-loop idea is wrong, and the plan needs rethinking.
11 Module 6 · Sidekicks

Make the check repeatable

Finding. My review habit is now a saved skill, review-checklist, with six checks. I tried a nine-check version and cut it back to the six I actually use.

The prompt I wrote:

I want to save a habit as something I can reuse. When I review a brief before it goes any further, here is what I actually check for:
- It names who owns it
- It says how we will know it worked
- The scope at the end matches the scope at the start
- It explains the problem before it proposes a fix
- Every claim is labeled confirmed, likely, or unknown, with a source
- Numbers name their time window (for example "late August" vs "since 12 Aug")

Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again. If one already exists, update it instead of making a new one. For each item, say Pass, Partly, or Missing, with a short reason and a quote from the brief. Then list the top 3 fixes. Do not invent facts or rewrite the brief unless I ask.
